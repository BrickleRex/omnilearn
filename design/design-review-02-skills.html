Decision 01

The shape of a skill project

Nine beats. Six have a twin in the code track; three are new because a skill has no compiler and needs reps.

0

Frame

Outcome + context: "5 meetings/mo, B2B SaaS, $0 budget, sent ~50 cold emails ever." Context filters every claim.

1

Map

The angle map, editable. Prune what you don't care about before any research spend.

2

Research

Harvest → assess → reconcile into claim cards. Paste your own sources at elevated trust.

3

Calibrate

Probes — plus "paste your last three cold emails" and get them graded. Existing work as calibration.

4

Learn

Deck + story units, every claim wearing a confidence chip and a citation. Dissent stays visible.

5

Drill

Short spaced reps: predict the A/B winner, 10 subject lines in 5 min, spot the mistake.

6

Make

The workspace: draft the real artifact with hints, ghost lines, watcher. Run = simulator.

7

Ship & learn

Send it for real, paste results back; prediction-vs-reality diff corrects the course.

8

Refresh

A freshness daemon re-scans sources; stale-evidence units get a re-verify badge.

The compiler question. Code gets honest feedback in milliseconds; cold email gets it in weeks, from a noisy world. So the track manufactures signal, ranked by honesty:

MOST HONESTPrediction drills

Real documented A/B results from the corpus. "These two subject lines were actually tested — which won?" Objective ground truth, instant, trains judgment.

FASTSimulator run

A persona panel built from the corpus reads your draft, plus a rubric grader whose rubric cites its evidence. Labelled as a model of the audience, never the audience.

SLOW, TRUEReal results

You ship, paste reply rate back, and the app diffs prediction vs reality — the one loop that can prove the course wrong for your context.

You've drafted a real email in the workspace. Cmd+Enter. Four ways the simulator can talk back (in the rail, same place the terminal lives on the code side):
